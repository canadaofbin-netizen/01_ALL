# Summary: Tang et al. (2023)

## Overview
**"No Person Is an Island: Unpacking the Work and After-Work Consequences of Interacting With Artificial Intelligence"** by Tang et al. (2023), published in the *Journal of Applied Psychology*, investigates how interactions with artificial intelligence impact employees' social affiliation needs, well-being, and behavior. Drawing on the [[Social_Affiliation_Model]] and attachment theory (specifically [[Attachment_Anxiety]]), the paper proposes that interacting with AI can lead to social deprivation because AI fails to provide the expected social feedback that humans intrinsically need.

## Key Findings
Across four studies (N=794) conducted in Taiwan, Indonesia, the US, and Malaysia using field surveys, field experiments, and simulations, the authors found a "double-edged sword" effect:
- **Adaptive Response:** Higher frequency of AI interaction increases the need for affiliation, which in turn leads to increased helping behavior toward coworkers.
- **Maladaptive Response:** Higher frequency of AI interaction also increases [[Workplace_Loneliness]], which leads to negative after-work consequences, specifically increased alcohol consumption and insomnia.
- **Moderation by Attachment Anxiety:** The effects along both pathways are stronger for employees with higher levels of [[Attachment_Anxiety]], as they are more sensitive to socially depriving interactions.
