# 1-s2.0-S0191886922000599-main.pdf

This research investigates how negatively keyed statements within multidimensional forced choice (MFC) personality measures affect reliability and criterion-related validity. Through Monte Carlo simulations and empirical data, the authors compare partially ipsative (PI) and Thurstonian Item Response Theory (TIRT) scoring methods. The findings suggest that TIRT scoring, particularly when using 20-40% highly discriminating heteropolar blocks, provides superior psychometric properties and criterion-related validity compared to PI scoring.

## Information

### Added from [[1-s2.0-S0191886922000599-main.pdf]] on 2026-08-01
- The Thurstonian Item Response Theory (TIRT) scoring method, combined with higher intrablock discrimination, yields better reliability and criterion-related validity than the partially ipsative (PI) scoring method. ([1-s2.0-S0191886922000599-main.pdf])
- Sufficient reliability and validity can be achieved by using highly discriminating 20-40% heteropolar blocks in conjunction with the TIRT model. ([1-s2.0-S0191886922000599-main.pdf])
- Criterion-related validity in MFC measures is significantly influenced by the proportion and design of heteropolar blocks. ([1-s2.0-S0191886922000599-main.pdf])
