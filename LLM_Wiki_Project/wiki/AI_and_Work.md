# AI and Work

The intersection of AI and work is a rapidly growing field of study examining how intelligent machines impact human workers. 

## Key Themes in Research
Based on the literature reviewed in the [[Kim_Lab_Journal_Club_Summer_2026]], research focuses on:
- **Productivity vs. Threat**: Reconciling evidence that AI dramatically boosts knowledge-worker performance while simultaneously increasing job insecurity and maladaptive behaviors (e.g., counterproductive work behavior).
- **Personality Interactions**: How individual differences, such as trait conscientiousness, shape whether employees benefit from or are harmed by interacting with AI (linking back to [[Trait_Activation_Theory]]). For instance, the orderliness subdimension of conscientiousness creates a non-complementary mismatch with intelligent machines, leading to increased role ambiguity and reduced performance for orderly employees (linking back to [[Complementarity_Theory]] and [[Role_Theory]]).
- **Trust and Bias**: How people perceive AI managers vs. human managers, often manifesting as [[Algorithm_Aversion]] or appreciation, and the moral psychology behind assigning responsibility to AI.
- **Control and Autonomy**: The rise of [[Algorithmic_Management]] and how "collaborating" with AI can either augment human creativity or restrict worker autonomy by manufacturing consent.
- **Well-being and Social Needs**: Examining how interaction with non-human systems like AI influences employees' social needs and well-being, often viewed through the lens of the [[Social_Affiliation_Model]].

## Empirical Findings
- **The "Jagged Technological Frontier"**: Dell'Acqua et al. (2026) demonstrated that generative AI acts as a massive productivity booster inside its capability frontier, but outside the frontier, it degrades performance due to [[Automation_Bias]] and overreliance.
- **Psychological Impacts**: Yam et al. (2023) demonstrated that employee exposure to robots (both physical and psychological) increases subjective [[Job_Insecurity]], which can drive maladaptive behaviors like [[Counterproductive_Work_Behavior]] (e.g., employee burnout and incivility).
- **Conscientiousness-Machine Mismatch**: [[Tang_Koopman_McClean_et_al_2022]] demonstrated across 3 studies that orderly (conscientious) employees experience *more* role ambiguity when working with intelligent machines (AI, robots, algorithms), which reduces their task performance—challenging the long-standing consensus about conscientiousness as the strongest performance predictor.
- **Social Affiliation and AI**: Tang et al. (2023) showed that employee interactions with AI trigger adaptive responses (increased need for affiliation → helping behavior) and maladaptive responses (increased [[Workplace_Loneliness]] → after-work alcohol consumption and insomnia), moderated by [[Attachment_Anxiety]]. This double-edged sword finding integrates with [[Social_Affiliation_Model]] theory.
