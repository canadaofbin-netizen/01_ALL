# Concept: Workplace Loneliness

## Definition
Workplace loneliness is a passive, maladaptive emotional state that arises when an employee's need for social connection and affiliation is not met by their interactions at work. It represents a discrepancy between a person's desired and actual social relationships within the organizational context.

## Key Details
Workplace loneliness often results from socially unfulfilling environments. Recent studies show that it can be exacerbated by modern work technologies. For instance, Tang et al. (2023) identified that frequent interactions with artificial intelligence can deprive employees of expected social feedback, triggering workplace loneliness as predicted by the [[Social_Affiliation_Model]]. 

This loneliness is highly consequential for employee well-being. It serves as a mediating mechanism that links workplace deprivation (e.g., interacting primarily with AI) to negative after-work outcomes, including increased alcohol consumption and insomnia. Gabriel et al. (2021) also noted that the detrimental effects of such loneliness are particularly likely to manifest during after-work hours. 

While distinct from threats like [[Job_Insecurity]], both represent psychological states that impair employee well-being and function.
