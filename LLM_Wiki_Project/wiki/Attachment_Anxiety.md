# Concept: Attachment Anxiety

## Definition
Attachment anxiety is a relatively stable individual trait rooted in attachment theory (Bowlby, 1969/1973; Simpson, 1990). It reflects an individual's heightened sensitivity to the absence or threat of loss of social connections, often characterized by a strong desire for closeness coupled with a fear of rejection or abandonment.

## Key Details
In the workplace context, attachment anxiety acts as a critical moderator for how individuals react to socially unfulfilling environments. As demonstrated in research by Tang et al. (2023), employees with higher levels of attachment anxiety are more sensitive to socially depriving interactions, such as extensive interaction with AI systems that fail to provide expected human-like social feedback. 

Because of this heightened sensitivity, individuals with high attachment anxiety experience amplified reactions under the [[Social_Affiliation_Model]]:
- **Stronger Adaptive Responses:** Even greater increases in the need for affiliation, driving more intense helping behaviors toward coworkers.
- **Stronger Maladaptive Responses:** Deeper feelings of [[Workplace_Loneliness]] and corresponding negative outcomes (e.g., insomnia, substance use).
