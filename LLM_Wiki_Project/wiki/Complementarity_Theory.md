# Concept

## Definition
Complementarity Theory (Carson, 1969; Kiesler, 1983) posits that individuals prefer interactions where there is a balance or complementary fit between their own characteristics and those of their interaction partners. Traditionally, this has been applied to human-to-human relationships, such as extraverted leaders working best with less extraverted employees.

## Key Details
- **Human-Machine Extension**: [[Pok_Man_Tang]] and colleagues (see [[Tang_Koopman_McClean_et_al_2022]]) extended complementarity theory to human-machine interactions.
- **The Conscientiousness Mismatch**: They found that highly orderly (conscientious) employees experience a non-complementary mismatch when working with intelligent machines. Because both the employee and the machine are highly systematic, rigid, and orderly, the lack of complementary traits (like flexibility or creativity) leads to negative outcomes such as increased role ambiguity.
