# Summary

## Overview
Tang, Koopman, McClean et al. (2022) challenge the traditional view that [[Conscientiousness]] is uniformly beneficial for job performance. Drawing on [[Complementarity_Theory]] and [[Role_Theory]], the authors argue that the orderliness subdimension of conscientiousness creates a non-complementary mismatch when employees interact with modern intelligent machines (AI, robots, algorithms), because both the machine and the employee are highly systematic and orderly.

## Key Points
- **Mismatch with Intelligent Machines**: While conscientious employees thrived with passive 20th-century technology, intelligent machines (which are inherently orderly) create a mismatch.
- **Three Studies**: The authors conducted three studies (ESM in Malaysia with AI, Field experiment in Taiwan with Robots, Online experiment in US with Algorithms), involving 681 participants.
- **Impact on Role Perceptions**: Working with intelligent machines increases both role breadth self-efficacy (especially for low-orderliness employees) and role ambiguity (especially for high-orderliness employees).
- **Performance Outcomes**: Increased role ambiguity leads to reduced task/service performance, demonstrating that conscientiousness/orderliness can actually harm performance in AI contexts.
